## The Assumption

What did you (or your team) *think* would be the case going in? What was the baseline expectation?

*Example (from an ISO 27001 implementation): "We assumed our 200-person org had documented most critical business processes. They've been operating 15 years; surely they have procedures written down."*

---

## The Reality

What actually happened? What did you discover that contradicted the assumption?

*Example: "During the first discovery workshop, we learned 40% of critical processes lived nowhere but in people's heads. Spreadsheets, ad-hoc notes, tribal knowledge. No formal procedures. Ever."*

---

## Why It Matters

Why was this gap important? What consequence did it create, or almost create?

*Example: "Without documented procedures, we couldn't assess against the standard, couldn't ensure consistency, and audit would have failed on missing evidence."*

---

## The Decision

What did you decide to do about it?

*Example: "We stopped the assessment for two weeks, sent a template for the 5 most critical processes, and built documentation into the monthly routine instead of treating it as a one-off project."*

---

## The Lesson

What should someone else in your field know? What's the takeaway? What would you do differently next time?

*Example: "Discovery isn't 'assess what you have.' It's 'discover what you actually need to document first.' Budget for that, don't assume it away."*
